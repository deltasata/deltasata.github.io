01 · Story

From the cosmos to the planet

I build machine-learning systems that find rare events in large streams of images and time series.

I have a PhD in astrophysics and 12+ years of analysing large, noisy data where the signal that matters is rare and every result has to hold up in peer review; 7+ of those years with hands-on machine learning.

Right now that means a real-time pipeline on Google Cloud built for ~10 million events (~1 TB) a night, and deep-learning models (transformers, ConvLSTMs, CNN ensembles) that pick out the handful that matter: about 70% of rare events caught while wrongly flagging only 1 in 10,000 ordinary ones.

I came to data from theory: theoretical physics and higher mathematics first, then code, statistics and data analysis. I like problems with no off-the-shelf method. Since 1996, the delay between a random signal and its overlapping copy had been thought impossible to find without a model of the signal. I showed that every wrong way to split the sum comes out rougher, turned that into a new statistic and proved why it works. With no template and no training data it finds hidden time-delayed copies at 92% precision under survey-grade noise (53% for the standard autocorrelation), confirmed on real data.

The data has been astronomical, but the problems are the ones industry has: irregular multi-band image sequences, sensor fusion, extreme class imbalance, predictions that must update with every new observation, and pipelines that must run unattended. They map directly onto Earth observation, forecasting, anomaly detection and computer vision.

I lead one of the rare-event searches in a 10,000-member international collaboration (working-group lead since 2025), aimed at some of its rarest and most demanding targets; my pipeline and models serve it. Alongside: generative models (diffusion, normalizing flows), simulation-based inference and LLM agents (EO-Agent).

Much of my time now goes into agentic AI for research: agents propose, run and log hundreds of experiments, while every claim has to pass quality checks I set in advance (pre-registered comparisons, repeated seeds, locked holdout data, explainability tests that catch false positives and shortcuts).

EO-Agent, conversational Earth-observation analyst

LLM agent that answers "was this place flooded, burnt or built on?" from free satellite images, every number checked against deterministic code. Research · paper on arXiv

OASIS, transformers for asynchronous image streams

Seven transformer architectures, chosen from 227 training runs of 100 model configurations in 19 architecture families and each trained on 10+ seeds, that score each new frame of an irregular multi-band image sequence: factorized vs joint space-time attention, windowed and pyramid variants and a Perceiver-style readout, with Time2Vec and causal masking; the best cut the recurrent baseline's loss by 30% and flags 70% of rare events at a 0.01% false-positive rate after 6 images, where the baseline needs 9; paper on arXiv (2610.17168).

Research · a new statistical method

Hidden echoes, a model-free statistic

A random signal overlapping with its own delayed copy admits infinitely many exact splits; since 1996 the delay was thought impossible to find without a model of the signal. Every wrong split comes out rougher: that became a new statistic, with a proof of why it works. 92% precision under survey-grade noise, against 53% for autocorrelation.

Research · 2019 – 2025 · new statistical methods and deep learning

Unresolved lensed supernovae, found from one blended light curve

When the copies of a lensed supernova are too close to separate, a survey sees one blended light curve, and most lensed supernovae will look like that. Six years of work: recognising them from the curve's shape with methods that need hardly any template (under 5% false positives, under 0.5% delay bias), then deep learning on the same problem: a 1D CNN and an LSTM.
