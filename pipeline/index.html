Real-time event pipeline · try it yourself

A flood of alerts every night. Find the few that matter.

Every night the Rubin Observatory's LSST survey sends out alerts about anything in the sky that changed. This pipeline pulls them from six public brokers, narrows them down to new objects worth a look, links each one to its neighbours in space and time, and fetches their full data. Watch how it works, then pick a time window and start a fresh, quick run of it yourself.

1 · How it works

Nine short scenes. Positions and light curves are drawn for illustration; every number is measured.

2 · Alerts in the last days

Alerts per UTC day, ending on the latest day with enough alerts.

3 · Your window, your run

Pick a start and a length (whole hours, up to 15 days). The counts below update instantly from the last main run; the button then starts a fresh, quick run of the pipeline for your window.

4 · Linking in space and time

Part B keeps a new object's neighbours: anything within x arcseconds on the sky and t days in time, even if it did not pass the filter. Move the limits and watch the set change. These are the real pairs of the last main run (names replaced by numbers).

5 · Every part, through every broker

Each broker can do each part; they differ in speed and limits. Single measurements of full-size runs.

6 · Why the numbers can differ

Three sets of numbers, made at different times by different routes. They answer slightly different questions, so they are not expected to match exactly.
